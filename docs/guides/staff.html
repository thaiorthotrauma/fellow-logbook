What the staff view is

The same logbook your fellows use, opened as a supervisor. You get a read-only list of every case logged by every fellow at your institution, and a PDF export that can be grouped three different ways. You do not log cases, and you cannot edit or delete a fellow's entry — the staff view has no New Entry tab at all.

It is built for the question a supervisor actually asks: what has this fellow been doing, and how much of it. The list answers it case by case; the export answers it as a summary you can take into a review meeting.

Two tabs

Institution Cases Every case logged by fellows at your institution, newest first, with the fellow's name on each card. Search, filter by where the case was done, sort, and narrow to one fellow.
PDF Export a month range as a formatted document — pooled across the institution, split fellow by fellow, or for one named fellow on their own.
What is different about your sign-in There is no email step and no verification code. You are recognised by your LINE identity alone, which your administrator registered for you in advance. If you have been given the staff rich menu, you are already set up.

Chapter 1

Getting in

Everything starts from the Fellow Logbook LINE Official Account. Staff see a different rich menu from fellows — two buttons instead of one.

Opening the staff view

  1. Open your chat with the Fellow Logbook Official Account.
  2. Tap Fellow Cases on the rich menu.
  3. The app opens on Institution Cases.

There is nothing to verify and nothing to remember. Your LINE identity is checked against the staff roster on the server each time you open it, and the header fills in with your name and institution.

You are in the right place if…

  • the header is amber, not teal;
  • it reads Staff view — read only under your institution;
  • there are two tabs — Institution Cases and PDF — and no New Entry.
Phone and tablet at the same time Each device links itself independently, so signing in on your tablet does not sign you out on your phone. Both stay linked, and you can use either without thinking about it.
Chapter 2

If it opens as something else

Four things can appear instead of the staff view. Each has a different cause, and only one of them needs your administrator.
A teal header with a New Entry tab You opened the app as a fellow rather than as staff — usually by using a bookmark or the default rich menu instead of the Fellow Cases button. Go back to the chat and tap Fellow Cases, which forces the staff view even for an account that also happens to be on the fellow roster.
An email verification screen Your LINE identity is not on the staff roster, so the app fell through to the fellow sign-in. Do not enter an email. Ask your administrator to register you; they will need your LINE user ID, which they can obtain by having you send any message to the Official Account.
"Open This in the LINE App" The link was opened in Safari or Chrome, or from a saved bookmark. The logbook runs only inside LINE's own in-app browser.
"Mobile & Tablet Only" You are on the desktop LINE client for Windows or Mac. The logbook is deliberately phone- and tablet-only. Open it on a phone or tablet.
"Something went wrong" A network or server problem rather than a rule. Tap Try again. If it keeps happening, tell your administrator — the failure is reported to them automatically, so they may already know.
Staff access takes effect immediately Unlike a fellow, you have no verification step to complete. The moment your administrator adds your LINE identity to the staff roster, the Fellow Cases button works — there is nothing to confirm from your side and nothing to wait for.

Getting registered, if you are not yet

Your administrator needs your LINE user ID — a long identifier you never see yourself and cannot read off your profile. Rather than hunting for it, you hand it over by messaging the bot:

  1. Open the chat with the Fellow Logbook Official Account.
  2. Send it any message at all — "hello" is enough. Nothing replies, and that is expected.
  3. Tell your administrator you have done it. Your message arrives in their admin channel with your LINE user ID attached, and they register you from there.

Once they confirm, tap Fellow Cases. If the button is not on your rich menu yet, close and reopen the chat — the staff menu is assigned to you individually, and it appears once it has been.

If you are both staff and a fellow Some supervisors are on both rosters. The Fellow Cases button is an explicit override: it always opens the staff view, even when you have an active fellow session. To reach your own fellow logbook instead, open the app from the default rich menu or a chat link.
Chapter 3

What you can see

Worth reading once, because the scope of the list is not quite the obvious thing, and two fields are withheld on purpose.

The scope is the fellow, not the theatre

You see every case logged by every fellow whose registered institution is yours — including the cases those fellows did elsewhere. A fellow of yours who operated at another hospital and tagged the case Outside institution still appears in your list.

It is not "cases that happened at this institution". A visiting fellow registered to another institution who operates in your theatre does not appear. The list follows the fellow, which is what makes it useful for supervision: it is the complete operative experience of the fellows you are responsible for.

Two things you will not find

The full HN

Hospital numbers arrive masked to their last four digits — ••••5512 in figure 3.1. This is enforced by the database function that serves the staff view, not hidden by the screen, so no part of the staff view can show you a full HN.

Four digits are enough to match a case against a list you already hold. They are not enough to look a patient up, which is the point.

Edit and Delete

An expanded case shows no action buttons. The staff view is read-only in the strict sense: there is no path through it that changes a fellow's record. If a case is wrong, the fellow who logged it is the one who corrects it.

Also absent There is no New Entry tab. Staff do not log cases, so the tab does not exist rather than existing and refusing.
Chapter 3 · continued

What you do see, in full

Everything except the HN and the two actions. A case in the staff view is the same record the fellow sees.
Who logged it The fellow's name, on the card and in the expanded detail — the one field the fellow's own view does not need, since every case there is theirs.
The clinical record Date, timing (official or after hours), place, the supervising staff surgeon, diagnosis, the AO/OTA code and any other classifications, approach, position, type of procedure, and the procedures performed.
The fellow's own account of their part Their role — primary surgeon, primary or secondary assist, observer, or uncertain — and the operative time. These two fields are what the export's charts and its pelvis table are built from.
Images Any films or intra-op photographs the fellow attached, loaded from the programme's private Drive when you expand the case. Tap a thumbnail for full size.
Memo Shown when the fellow wrote one. Bear in mind these are their private working notes rather than a formal part of the record — and they are excluded from every PDF export.

What the fellow sees of your reading

Nothing. Opening a case, searching, filtering, or exporting leaves no trace in the fellow's own view, and they are not notified. The staff view is a window, not an interaction.

If a case needs correcting Ask the fellow to open it in their own Case Log and use Edit case. They can change any field, including the role, at any point in the year — there is no lock-out date and no limit on how often. A correction made that way flows into every later export automatically.
Images are as the fellow captured them Nothing crops or redacts an attached image. If you open a film and find identifying detail visible in the frame, that is worth raising with the fellow directly — it is their responsibility at the point of capture, and the app cannot correct it afterwards.
Chapter 4

Institution Cases

One list, newest first, spanning every fellow at your institution. Three controls narrow it, and the count line always tells you whether you are looking at all of it.

Reading a card

Collapsed, each card gives you the date, a Home institution or Outside institution tag, the timing, the fellow's name, the diagnosis on one line, and the fellow's role with the operative time. The count above the list — "8 cases logged" — is your institution's running total.

Narrowing the list

  • Search matches diagnosis, other classification, approach, procedure, AO code, and region name. Whole words in any order; an AO code such as 2R3-C2 works as a term. It does not search the fellow's name — the badges do that.
  • All / Home / Outside filters by where the case was done. Use Outside to see at a glance how much of your fellows' experience is being gathered elsewhere.
  • Newest / Oldest flips the order. During a search, best matches lead instead, and this toggle resumes once the box is cleared.

Which control to reach for

"What has this fellow been doing?" Tap their badge. Everything else stays as it is, so you can combine it with Outside or a search term.
"How many acetabular cases have we logged?" Search the AO code or the word. The count line above the cards answers it without your opening anything.
"How much is being done away from here?" Clear the search and tap Outside. The count reports against the institution total — 2 of 8 cases.
Chapter 4 · continued

Filtering to one fellow

The badge row is the control you will use most, and it is the only way to filter by who logged a case — the search box does not look at names.

Each badge is a fellow, shortened to their first name plus the initial of their surname. Tap one to show only their cases; tap it again to clear. Badges start deselected, so the row is an opt-in narrowing rather than a set of exclusions — and selecting two shows both fellows together, which is the quickest way to compare them.

Only fellows who have logged at least one case appear. A newly registered fellow with no cases yet has no badge, which is also how you spot one who has not started logging.

Three quick reads worth knowing How much after-hours work? Look down the timing tags on the cards. How much away from home? Tap Outside and read the count line. How much pelvic and acetabular experience? Read the pelvis table on any export's summary page — it splits the totals by the role the fellow held.
Chapter 5

Opening a case

Tap any card to expand it in place. It scrolls itself to just under the header, so what you opened is what you are looking at. Tap it again to collapse.

Only one case is open at a time — opening a second closes the first. This is deliberate on a phone: it keeps the list scrollable instead of turning it into a wall of detail.

Fields the fellow left empty are simply absent rather than shown as blanks, so a short detail panel means a sparse case, not a display problem. Position and memo in particular are optional, and many cases will not carry them.

The list is loaded once per open Cases are fetched when the app starts, not continuously refreshed. If a fellow logs a case while you have the app open, reopen it to see it. Start a review meeting by reopening the app, so the numbers you quote are current.

Using the list in a review

Filter to the fellow with their badge, set Oldest, and scroll: you get their year in order, with the role they held on every case.

For anything longer than a quick look, export the PDF instead — the Single fellow grouping produces exactly this list with a summary page on the front, which is easier to read and easier to hand over.

Images are available to you too If the fellow attached films, the thumbnails load when you expand the case and open full size on a tap. They come from the programme's private Drive through the app — there is no shared link and nothing to request.
Chapter 6

Exporting a PDF

The same export engine the fellows use, with a grouping choice on top. The document is built on your own device and handed to your phone's native share sheet.

Exporting

  1. Open the PDF tab.
  2. Choose a grouping — By month range, By fellow, or Single fellow. The three are compared overleaf.
  3. From and To arrive set to the full span of your institution's cases. Narrow them for a quarter or a single month; only months with cases are offered.
  4. For Single fellow, pick the fellow from the dropdown that appears.
  5. Check the count line — "8 cases in range". If it reads 0, or if From is later than To, the button stays disabled and says which.
  6. Tap Generate PDF, then use the share sheet: Save to Files, mail it, or send it into a LINE chat.
Give it a moment An institution-wide export covers far more cases than one fellow's, so Building PDF… can sit for several seconds. Let it finish rather than tapping again.
The export ignores the list's filters A search or badge filter left running on the Institution Cases tab has no effect here. The export is always the complete record for the range and grouping you chose — narrow it with the month pickers and the grouping, not with the list.
Chapter 6 · continued

Which grouping to use

All three produce the same shape of document. What differs is how many summary pages it has, and whose cases sit under them.
By month range One pooled document for the whole institution: a single summary page across all fellows, then every case in date order, each tagged with the fellow who logged it. Use it for an institutional return or a caseload overview.
By fellow Each fellow gets their own summary page and their own case pages, one fellow after another in a single file. Use it when you need every fellow's individual record in one place — the end-of-year submission for the whole cohort.
Single fellow One named fellow, on their own, in the same shape as that fellow's own export. Use it for an individual review meeting, or when a fellow needs a countersigned record and you would rather generate it yourself than rely on theirs.

Files are named for the institution, the range, and the grouping — e.g. TOTS-staff-logbook_<institution>_2026-06_to_2027-05.pdf — so a folder of exports sorts itself.

Chapter 6 · continued

What the document contains

A summary page, then the cases in date order. Three things are left out of every grouping.
The summary page Case count for the range, top operated regions, the Experiences on Pelvis & Acetabulum table broken down by the role the fellow held, and pie charts of type of procedure, role, and place.
Then, one block per case Oldest first — the reverse of the list's default, because a logbook reads chronologically. Each block carries the date, the timing / place / AO-code chips, and the clinical fields.
Never included HN, the fellows' memos, and the images — in any grouping. The export is a record of operative experience, not a patient record, which is what makes it shareable as it stands.
Chapter 7

Logbook Demo

The second button on your rich menu. It exists so you can show a fellow the app without touching real data.

Logbook Demo opens the fellow experience — the same three tabs, the same fourteen questions — with nothing behind it. No account is created, no case is read, and nothing you do can be saved. A banner across the top of the form says exactly that.

Inputs are inert: you can scroll the whole form and see every question, including the AO body map, but nothing can be typed or selected. The Case Log shows its empty state, because no cases are ever fetched in demo mode, and the PDF tab reports that there is nothing to export.

Use it when you are explaining the form to an incoming fellow, or when you want to see a question from their side before asking them about it. Reload and it is exactly as it was.

Why the inputs are inert Not to restrict you — so that a walkthrough can never leave a stray half-finished case in anyone's logbook. Programme administrators have a fully interactive version of the same sandbox for testing the flow end to end.
A good way to use it Walk a new fellow through questions 6 and 7 in the demo before their first case. The AO picker is the only part of the form that is not self-evident, and it is much easier to explain on a screen than in words.

What the demo cannot show

  • The native date picker and the numeric keypad, since no field accepts focus.
  • Adding images, and the on-device conversion that comes with it.
  • The validation banner, which only appears when a save is attempted.
  • A populated Case Log or a real PDF export — both need saved cases, and the demo never creates any.

For those, walk the fellow through this guide's figures, or sit with them for their first real entry.

Chapter 8

Governance, and quick answers

Where the data lives, what is protected by construction rather than by convention, and where your own responsibility begins.

Where the data lives

Cases In the programme's database. Each fellow can read only their own rows; your staff view reaches them through a single dedicated database function that applies the institution scope and the HN masking together, so no screen can be added later that forgets either.
HN Masked to its last four digits before it reaches you, by that same function — not by the app on your phone. Never present in any export.
Images In the programme's own private Google Drive, fetched through the app when you expand a case. Never given a public link, and your device never talks to Google directly.
Exports Generated on your own device from cases already loaded — nothing is uploaded to build a PDF.
The export is where the app's protections end Once you save or send a PDF, it is an ordinary document on an ordinary device. It carries no HN, but it does carry fellows' names and the full clinical text of every case in range. Handle and share it the way your institution expects clinical records to be handled.

Quick answers

Can I correct a fellow's case? No — the staff view is read-only by design. Ask the fellow to edit it from their own Case Log; they can change any field at any time.
A fellow of mine is missing from the list. They appear once they log their first case, and only if their registered institution matches yours. If both are true and they are still absent, ask your administrator to check their roster entry.
Why can I see a case done at another hospital? Because the list follows the fellow, not the theatre. Your fellows' outside cases are part of their experience and are included, tagged Outside institution.
Can I see the full HN? Not through this app, by design. The masking is applied in the database, not on the screen.
Can I use my phone and my tablet? Yes, both at once. Each device links independently and neither signs the other out.
The app opened with a New Entry tab. You reached it as a fellow. Return to the chat and use the Fellow Cases button, which forces the staff view.
Nothing arrived in the LINE chat after exporting. Expected — the PDF goes to your phone's share sheet, not the chat. Use Save to Files, or choose a chat from the share sheet yourself.
The list will not load. Reopen the app from the rich menu. If it persists, tell your programme administrator; failures are reported to them automatically.
